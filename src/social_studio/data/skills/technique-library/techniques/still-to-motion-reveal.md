# still-to-motion-reveal

**Family:** reveal, camera. **Wants:** 1.5-2.5 s. **Source:** prompt-motion.com's gallery
(listudio-820c7b), studied 2026-10-09.

## Looks

A frame holds on what reads as a static photo, held just a beat too long to be sure, then it quietly starts
moving — fur ruffles, a camera does a slow push, a light shifts — revealing it was secretly a video clip all
along. A short caption names the shift (\"From stills to motion.\") without needing to explain it further,
since the frame already proved it.

## Build

- Open on a single frame of the clip, held static for 400-600ms (long enough to read as a photo) before
  playback starts, so the cut from "photo" to "video" is really just playback starting late.
- The giveaway motion should be subtle and physical (breathing, hair, a slow push-in), not an obvious effect,
  so the reveal feels like the subject was alive the whole time, not like a filter switching on.
- The caption fades in low in the frame, after motion has already started, so it confirms what the viewer
  already half-noticed rather than announcing it first.

## Sound

Silence or near-silence during the "photo" hold, ambient sound fading up the instant motion starts.

## Adapting

Works for any still-to-video feature claim. Keep the held "photo" window short — much past a second and the
viewer stops believing it was ever static.
